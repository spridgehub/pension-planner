You may already own themA global tracker is about 70% US and roughly a quarter in the same AI names. Adding an S&P 500 or Nasdaq fund on top doubles down on that bet.
Index funds and ETFsEvery index on this page has trackers on SIPP and ISA platforms with yearly charges from under 0.1% (US, UK) to 0.2 to 0.6% (single Asian countries). Check the fund's charge and whether it pays out or reinvests dividends.
CurrencyOverseas shares carry currency risk. A strong pound cuts your return; a weak pound adds to it, which often cushions a crash. Hedged share classes remove this at a small cost.
Near or in retirementA big fall just before or soon after you start drawing does the most damage (sequence risk). See the Retirement page's backtest for how a 60/40 plan held up.

Physical ETCs work in a SIPP or ISA; coins are the tax-free route outside them

Physical ETCsExchange-traded commodities backed by metal in a vault, such as iShares Physical Gold or Invesco Physical Gold. Allowed in SIPPs and ISAs. Charges about 0.1 to 0.25% a year. Silver and platinum versions exist.
CoinsBritannias and Sovereigns are UK legal tender, so they are free of Capital Gains Tax, and investment gold carries no VAT. They cannot go in a pension. Silver coins and bars carry 20% VAT when bought new.
Miners are sharesGold-mining funds rise and fall harder than gold and also fall with the stock market. They are an equity bet with a gold flavour.
No incomeMetals pay no interest or dividends, so all of the return is the price. That makes them a diversifier, not a source of retirement income. Many multi-asset funds hold none to about 10%.
