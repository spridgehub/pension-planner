Volatility is how much the price typically swings in a year, measured from daily moves. 30% means a typical year lands within about 30% either side of where it started. When the 1-month figure is well above the 1-year one, the stock is in a stormy patch; history says storms pass but cluster.

Ranges assume moves of the size seen over the last year, with no drift: one typical move either side holds about 2 in 3 outcomes, two moves about 19 in 20. "Held" checks the idea against each stock's own past: how often it actually stayed inside its 3-month range, measured with the volatility known at the time (about 68% would mean the yardstick works). Real markets have fatter tails: big jumps on results days happen more often than the bell curve says.
